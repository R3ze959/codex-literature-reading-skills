# Figure assets and spoken copy

Read for actual crop delivery or when polishing figure-side text. Do not generate assets merely because this reference is loaded.

## Original figure crops

- Crop from the original PDF or supplied original image. Remove unrelated page furniture while retaining panel labels, axes, legends, scale/color bars, and annotations needed to interpret the data.
- Prefer a complete figure when it remains legible. If the user requests complete figures, include the full published figure group. For selective panel crops, use natural panel boundaries and retain the context needed to interpret them; never cut a panel in half.
- Keep captions/source locators in the outline even when outside the image crop. Distinguish PDF page indices from printed page labels.
- Preserve vector content when practical; otherwise render sufficient resolution for the intended slide size, commonly 220–300 dpi. Inspect the actual crop rather than assuming a page-render command captured all labels.
- Name assets by slide/source/figure, for example `slide05_p03_fig2ab.png`, with a mapping back to the original page. Keep scratch page renders separate from delivered crops.
- Label adaptations or reconstructed diagrams `根据原文数据整理` or `根据文献归纳绘制`. Honor original-only requests without substituting generated visuals.

## Grounded slide copy

Prefer `source + sample/measurement + reported value or visible observation + implication under its conditions`. Use qualitative wording when an exact number is not available; mark image estimates as approximate.

Keep on-slide text short and put explanations in speaker notes. A useful spoken paragraph connects the comparison to the claim, explains the most informative observation, and states any limitation that changes the interpretation. Avoid repeatedly announcing a template such as “这页的核心是” or using broad praise without a concrete source detail.

Match the user's preferred voice and format. Distinguish hypotheses with language such as `作者解释为`, `可能`, or `仍需验证` when warranted. Do not make every sentence hedged when it is a directly reported result.
